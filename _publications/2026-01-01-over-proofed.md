"Why do some authoritarian leaders who have systematically weakened their own militaries through purges still deploy those militaries abroad? This article argues that the relationship between coup-proofing and foreign military intervention is nonlinear, but in a form that prior theory has not specified: purging military officers increases the probability of foreign intervention up to a moderate threshold, then decreases it. Using the Military Purges in Dictatorships (MPD) dataset (Sudduth, 2021) with the International Military Intervention (IMI) dataset (Pickering &amp; Kisangani, 2009; Pearson &amp; Baumann, 1993) across 114 autocracies from 1965 to 2005, we show that officer purges predicts combat interventions abroad in a curvilinear pattern. Crucially, this effect is driven almost entirely by purges of middle-rank officers not top commanders or junior soldiers. Purging 2-3 generals or colonels in a rolling three-year window is associated with a peak probability of foreign combat deployment; purging more than that predicts declining engagement."
